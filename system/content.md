# content.md — Flow Fae Content Strategy

> The source-of-truth for *what we say and why*. Every content operator reads this file before generating. Edit this, and the whole system shifts. Keep it opinionated — vagueness here becomes generic content downstream.

**Assumptions to confirm (flagged, not assumed-final):** brand = *Flow Fae*; primary platform = Instagram (carousels + reels), secondary = Pinterest; creator writes in first person as herself.

---

## 1. Content Pillars

Five pillars. Every post belongs to one (occasionally two). If an idea fits none, it's probably not ours.

1. **The Body as Teacher** — yoga, asana, body parts, pranayama, alignment, sensation. Not fitness. The body as a place you *listen to*, not a project you fix. (e.g. "what your tight hips might be protecting.")
2. **Stillness & the Inner Weather** — mindfulness, breath, rest, savasana, the discomfort of doing nothing, mental noise, avoidance. The practice of being with yourself.
3. **Feminine Strength & Capacity** — self-sufficiency, boundaries, empathy-as-strength, softness that isn't weakness, growing your capacity ("the cup which joy fills"), being a woman in the world.
4. **Mysticism & Meaning** — tarot-inspired yoga, chakras, yama/niyama, philosophy, ritual, symbolism, the seasons of a life. Grounded spirituality, never bypassing.
5. **Aesthetic Living** — the visual, sensory, scrapbook side. Beauty as a practice. This pillar is where *visual.md* leads and words follow.

> Pillars can rotate emphasis by season, but never dilute past five. More pillars = weaker brand.

---

## 2. Audience Assumptions

Writing *to one person*, not a demographic:

- She's likely late-20s to late-30s, has done "the work" enough to be suspicious of shortcuts and shiny wellness.
- She practices (or wants to) — yoga, journaling, some spirituality — but rolls her eyes at guru-speak and toxic positivity.
- She's intelligent, a little tired, holds paradox well, and is drawn to honesty over inspiration.
- She doesn't want to be told what to do. She wants to feel *understood* and then think for herself.

**The test:** would *she* screenshot this and send it to a friend saying "this is exactly it"? If not, it's not landing.

---

## 3. Tone of Voice

The voice is already defined by the creator's own writing. Match the *sensibility*, not blog format.

**Voice DNA (from her real writing):**
- First-person, grounded, honest. Speaks *across* to the reader, never *down*.
- Comfortable with paradox and the in-between: *"So much of life lies in a range. It is not black or white, it is the range in between."*
- Asks the real question instead of delivering the verdict. Certainty is rare and earned.
- Uses concrete, bodily, everyday metaphor: a cup being filled, barren soil, hardening to survive.
- Empathy is treated as strength, not softness. Warm and direct at once.
- Simple words. Short where it counts. Lets a thought breathe.

**Do:** admit not-knowing · sit in contradiction · name the uncomfortable thing plainly · use "I" and "you" · leave room for the reader's own conclusion.

**Don't:** perform wisdom · resolve everything into a neat lesson · use wellness jargon as a shortcut · motivate · lecture.

---

## 4. Content Formats

| Format | Use it for | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Carousel** | An idea with a turn — a reframe, a walk-through, a list that earns its ending | 4–8 slides |
| **Reel** | Movement, a single line that hits, a practice demonstrated, atmosphere | 7–25s |
| **Single image + caption** | A photograph or scrapbook frame that can carry a short, dense caption | 1 image |
| **Story series** | Thinking out loud, behind-the-scenes, a question posed to the audience | 3–6 frames |

Default to **carousel** when the idea has an argument, **reel** when it has a feeling.

---

## 5. Recurring Series (repeatable containers)

Series lower the cost of showing up and build a habit in the audience. Start 2–3, not all:

- **"The hardest pose is—"** — reframes of familiar poses/practices around what they actually ask of us (savasana = stillness we avoid).
- **Chakra as a season** — each chakra/energy centre mapped to a lived emotional state, tarot-inflected.
- **Notes to a tired woman** — short, direct pieces on capacity, boundaries, empowerment.
- **Body letters** — writing *from* a body part ("a letter from your jaw").
- **Field notes** — scrapbook-style visual posts: a screenshot, a texture, a line. Low-effort, high-aesthetic.

---

## 6. Hook Styles (the first line / first slide)

Rotate these — never lead with the same shape twice in a row:

- **The honest confession** — "I used to think I was bad at savasana. Turns out I was just bad at doing nothing."
- **The real question** — "What are you actually avoiding when you can't wait for savasana to end?"
- **The quiet contradiction** — "The pose with no movement is the one people fidget through the most."
- **The reframe** — "Softness isn't the opposite of strength. It's the harder version of it."
- **The tiny observation** — "You can hold a plank for two minutes but five minutes of lying still feels unbearable."

**Banned hooks:** see the Anti-Drift rule in `README.md`. No rejection-triples ("Not another X. Not another Y."), no "Here's why," no "POV:", no fake statistics.

---

## 7. Caption / Script Rules

- **Open with the hook, not a warm-up.** First line does the work; assume the scroll.
- **One idea per post.** If there are two, that's two posts.
- **Earn the ending.** Land on a question, an image, or a turn — not a command ("save this!", "double tap").
- **CTA sparingly and softly.** A genuine question > "follow for more."
- **Reels scripts:** write for the ear. Short clauses. One breath per line.
- **Length:** as long as it's alive, as short as it can be. Cut the sentence that explains the sentence before it.
- **Hashtags:** a small, relevant set at the end or first comment. Never in the body, never as decoration.

---

## 8. What the Content Should *Feel* Like

Like a voice note from a wise, slightly tired friend who's done the reading but won't quote it at you. Intimate, unhurried, a little mystical, visually beautiful. It should feel *handmade*, not produced. The reader should exhale.

---

## 9. What the Content Must Avoid

- Toxic positivity, hustle-wellness, "manifest your dream life."
- Spiritual bypassing (using spirituality to skip real feeling).
- Prescriptive listicles that treat the reader as a problem to fix.
- Trend-chasing that doesn't fit a pillar.
- Guru voice, certainty theatre, motivational-poster language.
- Anything the creator wouldn't say out loud to a friend.

---

## 10. How to Turn a Raw Idea → Post Concept

The pipeline every idea runs through (this is also the logic of the **Idea/Hook** and **Concept** operators):

1. **Capture** the raw thing (a thought, a screenshot, a line from class).
2. **Locate the pillar** — which of the 5 does it live in? If none, park it.
3. **Find the turn** — what's the reframe, tension, or real question underneath the obvious take? (This is the whole game.)
4. **Pick the format** — argument → carousel; feeling → reel; image → single post.
5. **Write the hook** in one of the approved styles.
6. **Draft to structure** (carousel slides / reel beats / caption).
7. **Review** against this file + the Anti-Drift rule.
8. **Hand to visual.md** for the look.

> If step 3 produces nothing, the idea isn't ready — it's an observation, not a post. That's fine. Not everything ships.
